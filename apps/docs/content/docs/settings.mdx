Open the settings page from the extension's **Options** entry, for example by right-clicking the toolbar icon in Chrome and choosing **Options**. Changes save as you make them.

## Using the settings page

- The page opens on **Essentials**, the few settings most people change. Every other tab is listed under **Advanced**, and each setting on **Essentials** is also in its own tab there: changing it in either place changes the same setting.
- **Search settings...** at the top finds a setting in any tab and says how many match. Every word you type must match, in any order, ignoring capitals and accents. The words are highlighted where they matched, the tabs with matches show their count highlighted, and a short note under a setting says where it matched when that text is not shown, such as its description or its setting ID. Some settings are also found by other words they are known by, such as night mode for **Theme**. What it looks in, how words match, and what it shows can be changed in [Settings Search](#settings-search).
- Each tab is split into titled sections. A tool's on/off switch sits next to its heading; while it is off, the rest of its settings are folded away. Technical settings, such as timeouts and file name lengths, are under **Advanced settings**, which stays closed until you open it or a search finds something in it.
- A changed setting is marked **Modified**, with a button to reset it to its default. **Reset All** resets every setting.
- **Back up settings** saves a backup file with the parts you select: settings, this computer's settings, saved searches and the table view, prompts and About me, smart rules, AI services (with **Base URL** and provider-specific fields), covers, and, only when you select them and confirm, the AI service keys, protected with a password by default. **Restore settings** shows what each part of a backup would change, lets you pick parts and **Merge** or **Replace**, and keeps a safety copy for **Undo import**; it also merges a plain settings file onto your settings. Neither touches your bookmarks. **Export Bookmarks** next to them opens the bookmarks manager on its export tool. See [Back up and restore settings](/guides/import-export#back-up-and-restore-settings).
- Settings you change are stored in the browser's sync storage, so they follow you to other devices when browser sync is on. Settings that suit one screen or name something that differs on each computer stay on the device where you change them: the layout of the popup and the bookmark manager, the manager and popup shortcuts, smart rules, storage limits, taking covers while browsing or with quick save, the folder chosen for quick save, AI activity recording, Ask AI's web search, and local AI agents. Each of them says so below (**Kept on this device only**, or **for this device only**). AI services, with their API keys, **Base URL**, and **Extra Headers JSON**, also stay on this device. See [Privacy](/privacy).
- In the lists you check and reorder (the popup's parts, buttons, and hover card details, card and preview details, sidebar tools, menus, and right-click menu entries), anything a later version adds shows until you hide it, and what you hid stays hidden.

## Saving & Search [#search]

### Folder Matches [#folder-matches]

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Folder Matches** | On | While you search, list the folders whose names match above the results, to save the current page there |
| **Maximum Folder Matches** | <SettingDefault path="search.folder_matches.max" /> | How many matching folders to list |
| **Folder Name Weight** | <SettingDefault path="search.folder_matches.name_weight" /> | How much a word in the folder's own name counts |
| **Parent Folders Weight** | <SettingDefault path="search.folder_matches.parent_weight" /> | How much a word in the names of the folders it is in counts, so "react hooks" finds Hooks inside React |
| **Bookmarks Inside Weight** | <SettingDefault path="search.folder_matches.bookmark_weight" /> | How much a word in the titles and sites of the bookmarks in the folder counts. 0 searches folder names only |

### Search Matching [#search-matching]

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fuzzy Matching** | On | Words spelled a little differently match too, so "rect" finds React. Results are ranked by relevance (BM25), and closer spellings rank higher |
| **One Edit From** | <SettingDefault path="search.search_matching.one_edit" /> | Shortest word, in letters, that may differ by one edit: a letter added, removed, or changed, or two swapped |
| **Two Edits From** | <SettingDefault path="search.search_matching.two_edits" /> | Shortest word, in letters, that may differ by two edits |
| **Match Word Starts** | On | A word you type also matches longer words it starts, so "rea" finds React while you type. A whole word still ranks higher |
| **Term Saturation (BM25 k1)** | <SettingDefault path="search.search_matching.bm25_k1" /> | How much a word that appears again keeps adding to a result's score. Lower makes one mention count almost as much as many |
| **Length Normalization (BM25 b)** | <SettingDefault path="search.search_matching.bm25_b" /> | How much shorter results are favored over longer ones with the same words: 0 not at all, 1 fully |

### Bookmark manager menus [#manager-menus]

Arrange each of the manager's menus on its own tab: the **Item menu** (right-click a row or a folder in the tree, or a row's ⋯ button), the **Organize menu** (⋮ in the header), the **Empty space menu** (right-click below the rows), and the **Preview buttons** (under a bookmark's preview, the first one the main button). Uncheck a command to hide it; it moves to **Hidden**, where checking it again adds it back at the end. Drag a command by its handle to reorder it, or drag a **Section break** to move commands from one section to another; the handle is also a button whose menu moves the item to the top, up, down, or to the bottom, so the keyboard works too. **Add section break** adds one at the end for you to move up, and its ✕ removes it, joining two sections. **Restore defaults** brings back that menu alone. Every menu keeps at least one command, and the item menu keeps **View Details** or **Copy Bookmark ID**, so it has a command for any bookmark or folder; where none of the commands you kept applies, the menu says **No actions available**. Every menu syncs with your other settings.

### Popup menus [#popup-menus]

Choose which commands a bookmark and a folder offer in the popup and side panel, and drag a command by its handle to reorder it within its group, as with the manager's menus. A row's first commands are buttons that show while you point at it or move to it with the keyboard: **Delete** on a bookmark, and **Expand all subfolders**, **Add current page**, **Add folder**, and **Delete** on a folder. The rest, such as **Open in new tab** and **Open in new window**, are in the row's ⋯ menu, and right-clicking a row (or its menu key) lists them all. Hide a button to make the next command a button instead. **Restore defaults** brings back the full menus. **Open in new tab** or **Delete** always stays for bookmarks, and **Add folder** or **Open all** for folders. Syncs with your other settings, like the manager's menus.

### Manager and popup shortcuts [#page-shortcuts]

Change the keys that work inside the bookmark manager, the popup, and the side panel; [Keyboard shortcuts](/guides/keyboard-shortcuts) lists the keys they start with. Click **Change** beside a shortcut and press the new keys, or `Esc` to cancel. Keys another shortcut of the same page already has are refused with that shortcut's name, and so are keys the browser keeps and the keys that move through lists (the arrows, `Home`, `End`, and `Space`, alone or with `Shift`, `Ctrl`, or `Cmd`, and `Enter`), unless they are the shortcut's own. The popup and side panel shortcuts never use `Ctrl` or `Cmd`. The reset button beside a changed shortcut gives it its default keys back, and **Reset all shortcuts** does that for every shortcut. Copy, cut, and paste keep the browser's keys, and the [quick save shortcuts](#quick-save) belong to the browser. Kept on this device only.

## AI [#ai]

AI is off by default. These settings matter only after you turn on **AI Features**; until then, the AI settings here and on the **AI Tools** tab are greyed out under a note saying so. You can still change them. Until the default service is set up, an **AI is optional** box at the top says what AI does, what it needs, what it may cost, and what is sent, with a link to [Set up AI tools](/guides/ai-tools).

| Setting | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI Features** | Off | Turn on the AI tools and the popup's folder recommendations. In Firefox, turning it on asks for your consent to share your API key, bookmarks, and the current page's title and URL with your AI provider |
| **AI services** | None | The providers you add, each with its own model and key; AI tools use the default service, except that **Built-in AI** only suggests folders: while it is the default, the other tools use the first other service that is turned on |

Below these, the provider panel holds **API Key**, **Base URL**, **Custom Model**, and **Extra Headers JSON**, with **Verify Service** and **Refresh Models**. The folder limits are sent to the provider as instructions, and its output is not checked against them. See [Set up AI tools](/guides/ai-tools).

**Requires an API key**, under **API Key**, starts the way the provider expects. Turn it off to use a service without a key, such as one behind a proxy or gateway that adds the key itself.

### About me [#about-me]

**About me** (empty by default) is a few words about you, such as "I'm a teacher; I save lesson material", up to 1,000 characters. While it has text, the built-in prompts of every AI feature send it to your AI provider, so results fit you better; a custom prompt sends it only if it uses `{{aboutMe}}`. It syncs through your browser account and is saved when you leave the field.

### What Ask AI may do [#ask-ai-actions]

Every lookup and change Ask AI can make is listed here, and all are on by default. Turn one off and Ask AI is not offered it at all. Applying a plan of changes always asks you first, whatever you choose here.

### AI activity [#ai-activity]

**Record requests** (off by default, for this device only) keeps the last 50 AI requests on this device, with API keys and credential headers removed, so you can see exactly what was sent. Turning it off deletes the log. See [See what was sent](/guides/ai-tools#see-what-was-sent).

## For developers [#developers]

### Local AI agents [#agent-bridge]

**Allow local AI agents** (off by default, for this device only) lets AI agents on your computer, such as Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor, look up your bookmarks through the bookmark-scout-mcp program, which you install and register with `bookmark-scout-mcp install`. It does not need **AI Features**. Turning it on asks your browser to let the extension talk to that program. **Allow changes** (off by default) also lets them save pages and propose moves, renames, deletions, and new folders; each change opens a Bookmark Scout window where you approve or decline it, and you can undo it in the bookmark manager. The panel below the switch shows whether the program is running.

**Hide private values** (on by default) replaces sensitive URL parameters, tokens, and email addresses in the titles and URLs agents receive, using the Privacy Scanner's list. **What local agents may do** lists every lookup, all on by default; one you turn off is not offered to agents at all.
