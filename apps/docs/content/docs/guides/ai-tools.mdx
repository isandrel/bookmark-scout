---
title: Set up AI tools
description: Turn on the optional AI features, choose a provider and model, add your API key, and know exactly what bookmark data each AI tool sends.
---

AI features are **optional and off by default**, and Bookmark Scout works fully without them. Bookmark Scout has no AI service of its own. To use AI, you need:

- **An account with an AI company**, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google. Settings call this company the **provider**. Most providers charge for each use, usually a small amount; check their prices before you start. An AI program that runs on your own computer, such as Ollama, costs nothing but needs a fairly powerful computer.
- **An API key**: a secret code from your account page at that company that lets Bookmark Scout use your account. Keep it private, like a password.

You also pick a **model**: the AI version the company offers, such as a faster or a smarter one. When AI is on, requests go directly from your browser to the provider you chose, with your key.

For folder suggestions only, Chrome can do without an account or a key: see [Free AI on this computer](#free-ai-on-this-computer).

<Callout type="warn" title="Before you turn AI on">
AI tools send bookmark titles, URLs, and folder names or paths to the provider you configure. The provider handles that data under its own terms, and API use may cost money. Review every suggestion before you apply it.
</Callout>

## Turn on AI

<Steps>
<Step>
Open Bookmark Scout's settings (in Chrome, right-click the toolbar icon and choose **Options**) and go to the **AI** tab. Until a service is set up, a box at the top, **AI is optional**, sums up what AI does, what it needs, what it may cost, and what is sent.
</Step>
<Step>
Turn on **AI Features**.
</Step>
<Step>
Under **AI services**, click **Add service** and pick a provider. The picker lists well-known providers under **Featured**, then **Local servers** and **Custom**; type in its search box to find about 200 more under **More providers (models.dev)**. Then choose an **AI Model**.
</Step>
<Step>
Enter your **API Key** if the provider needs one. For the featured providers, **Get an API key from** the provider opens the page where you create one after signing in. The key is saved when you leave the field, and stored only on this device. A key that does not match the provider's format is rejected and not saved.
</Step>
<Step>
Optional: click **Refresh Models** to load the provider's current model list, or **Verify Service** to check that the provider is reachable, accepts the key, and offers the chosen model. Both read the provider's model list, which costs nothing; Verify only sends a short test prompt to providers without a list, such as Azure OpenAI. The browser asks for access to that one provider's address the first time. A refreshed list is kept for the model picker until you refresh again.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Screenshot name="options-ai" />

The AI buttons in the popup and side panel appear once AI Features is on and the default service is set up (with its key, if the provider needs one). Until then, the popup shows **AI is on but not set up yet** with **Finish AI setup**, which opens this tab, and the AI tools in the bookmarks manager stay disabled.

Settings save automatically. AI services, with their API keys, **Base URL**, and **Extra Headers JSON**, stay in this browser's local storage and are never synced. Other AI settings, such as whether AI is on, sync through your browser account where the browser supports it.

## Free AI on this computer

In Chrome, Bookmark Scout can use **Chrome's built-in AI**, a small model (Gemini Nano) that runs on your computer: no account, no API key, no cost, and nothing leaves your computer. It suggests folders only.

<Steps>
<Step>
On the **AI** tab, click **Use built-in AI** under **Free on this computer**, at the top of the **AI is optional** box. **Built-in AI (on this computer)** is also first in **Add service**. Both appear only where Chrome can run the model.
</Step>
<Step>
Click **Download the model**. Chrome downloads it once: about 4 GB on a computer with a graphics card, 2.7 GB without one. When the service says **Ready**, the model is on this computer. If you close Settings while it downloads, Chrome keeps downloading in the background.
</Step>
<Step>
Turn on **AI Features** if it is off: adding the service does not turn AI on.
</Step>
</Steps>

- Built-in AI needs Chrome 138 or later on macOS 13 or later, Windows 10 or 11, Linux, or a Chromebook Plus, with 22 GB of free disk space and a graphics card with more than 4 GB of memory, or 16 GB of memory and 4 processor cores. You do not have to check this yourself: **Check this computer**, under the service (or in the **Free on this computer** box), shows your Chrome version, operating system, memory, and processor cores, and names what a browser cannot see (free disk space and graphics memory). When Chrome says the model cannot run here but nothing rules your computer out, it opens Chrome's flag for you: set it to **Enabled BypassPerfRequirement**, restart Chrome, and click **Check again**. Where your organization turned it off, the service says so. Firefox has no built-in AI.
- It only suggests folders: in the popup and side panel, and for the AI quick save shortcut. Ask AI, Auto-Tagging, Content Summarizer, and AI Folder Reorganization use your other service that is turned on, or one you add.
- It reads the page's title and address, the names of the folders that best match it (a short list picked on your device), and with **Read page content** on, the start of the page's text. It answers only with folders from that list.
- It works best with English, Japanese, Spanish, German, and French. When the model fails or takes too long, the suggestions come from a plain word match on your device instead.
- The popup and the AI quick save shortcut never start the download: until the model is ready, the popup shows **Download the built-in AI model in Settings** instead of the AI buttons, and quick save uses the best match below.
- AI quick save keeps the model ready for 5 minutes after a save, so the next one is quicker. When the model is not ready, fails, or takes too long, the page is saved to the folder that best matches it, as the **Best match** setting does.
- With **Record requests** on, each call appears in **AI activity** marked **On device**.

## Use more than one service

Save as many services as you like, even several of the same provider: for example a work proxy and a home proxy, each with its own key, address, and model.

- Every AI tool uses the **Default** service. Choose **Set as default** in a service's menu, or, when more than one service is turned on, pick one from the arrow next to the sparkle button in the popup.
- **Built-in AI** only suggests folders: while it is the default, the other AI tools use the first other service that is turned on. Drag a service by its handle in the list to choose which one comes first.
- Turn a service off with its switch to hide it from the popup without deleting it.
- **Duplicate** copies a service, key included. **Delete** removes it and its key from this device. Built-in AI can be added only once, so it has no **Duplicate**.
- **Base URL**, **Extra Headers JSON**, and provider-specific fields such as OpenAI's organization sit under **More settings**.

The **AI Provider** picker has a search box. The extra providers come from a copy of the open [models.dev](https://models.dev) catalog that ships with the extension, so choosing one sends nothing to models.dev; requests go only to the provider you pick.

**Base URL** overrides the provider's address for every request, for example to use a proxy. It must start with `http://` or `https://`. **Extra Headers JSON** adds headers to every request, and **Custom Model** sets a model ID for providers that accept any model name.

### Local servers

Local servers must accept requests from the extension:

- **Ollama:** set `OLLAMA_ORIGINS=chrome-extension://*,moz-extension://*` and restart Ollama. Without it, Ollama answers 403 and Verify says the server refused the extension.
- **LM Studio:** turn on **Enable CORS** in the server settings, or start it with `lms server start --cors`.
- **LocalAI:** set an API key; its CSRF check rejects keyless requests from the browser.
- **text-generation-webui:** start it with `--api`.
- **CLIProxyAPI:** accepts the extension's requests as configured by default. Start it before you click **Verify Service**; if it is not running, Verify says it could not reach `localhost:8317`.

### When a check fails

Verify and Refresh name the likely cause: a rejected key (401, or 400 from Google and xAI), a wrong Base URL (404), a provider that is rate-limiting requests (429, which still means the key works), a server that refused the extension's origin, no answer within 8 seconds, or an address that does not serve a provider API.

## Write your own prompts

Each AI tool starts with a built-in prompt. To change how a tool behaves, go to **Settings**, then **AI Tools**, then **Prompts**:

1. To read a tool's built-in prompt first, click the eye icon next to it. Click **Customize**, here or in the viewer, and the editor opens with the built-in prompt to start from.
2. Edit the text. Click a variable such as `{{maxTags}}` to insert it; the tool fills it in from your settings. **Preview with current settings** shows the result. Or type `{{` and pick one from the list, which shows what each placeholder is and its value now. `{{today}}`, `{{language}}`, and `{{aboutMe}}` work in every tool, and `{{existingTags}}` in Auto-Tagging; a placeholder with nothing to fill in, such as `{{aboutMe}}` while About me is empty, is left out with its line. Write `\{{` for literal braces. A placeholder the tool does not fill in is flagged, with a suggestion when it looks like a typo, and **Reset to built-in prompt** starts over.
3. Click **Save prompt**. The new prompt becomes the tool's prompt.

Keep several prompts per tool, up to 10 in all, and switch between them with the tool's menu; **Default (built in)** goes back to the original. Your prompts sync across your devices through your browser account, so each one must stay under 7 KB. Bookmark Scout adds its own output format rules after your prompt, so results still load even if your prompt asks for something else. It also adds a line asking for the language set in **AI output language** (**Settings**, then **AI**); in your own prompt, `{{language}}` names that language.

## What each tool sends

| Tool | Where | Sends to the provider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Folder recommendations | Popup, the sparkle button next to the search box (shown only when AI is on) | The current page's title and URL, and your bookmark folder names. With **Read page content** on, also the page's main text. With Built-in AI, nothing leaves your computer |
| **Auto-Tagging** | Manager, Tools, **AI** | Titles, URLs, and folder paths of the bookmarks in the current folder, and with **Read page content** on, each page's main text; with **Send Existing Tags** on (the default), also the names of your most used tags |
| **Content Summarizer** | Manager, Tools, **AI** | Titles, URLs, and folder paths of the bookmarks in the current folder, and with **Read page content** on, each page's main text |
| **AI Folder Reorganization** | Manager, Tools, **AI** | Titles, URLs, and folder paths of the bookmarks in the scope you choose |
| **Ask AI** | Popup and side panel, the chat button next to the search box (shown only when AI is on) | Your messages and the conversation so far, and what it looks up: matching bookmarks (with their folders, dates added, tags, and summaries), folder names, the current page's title and URL, whether bookmarked links it checks still work, and with **Read page content** on, the text of pages it reads |
| **AI Context Packer** | Manager, Tools, **AI** | Nothing. It saves a file on your computer and works with AI turned off. |

Every AI feature that uses a provider also sends your **About me** text (**AI** settings) while it has any; it is empty by default.

Turn on **Auto-recommend on Open** to request folder recommendations every time the popup opens. Leave it off to send a request only when you click the button.

## Read page content

Titles and URLs do not always say what a page is about. Turn on **Read page content** in **Settings**, then **AI**, and folder recommendations, **Auto-Tagging**, and **Content Summarizer** also send the main text of each page. Turning it on asks for access to websites; if you decline, it stays off.

- Bookmark Scout downloads each page itself, without your cookies, so you get the logged-out version of a site. It keeps the article text without menus, scripts, images, or link addresses.
- Each page sends up to about 3,000 characters, and one tool run reads up to 40 pages. Later bookmarks in a large folder are sent with their title and URL only, as are pages that do not load.
- Pages on your local network, such as `localhost`, private IP addresses, and `.local` names, are never read, so intranet pages do not reach a cloud provider.
- Page text comes from the web and could contain instructions aimed at the AI. Bookmark Scout tells the model to treat it only as data, and you still review every suggestion before it is saved.

## Ask AI

Click the chat button next to the search box in the popup or side panel to ask about your bookmarks, the page you are on, or anything else. It uses your default AI service; switch services from the menu in its header.

- To answer, the AI can search your bookmarks (also by folder, tag, site, or date added), list your folders, look up bookmarks' tags and summaries, find duplicates, suggest folders for a page, check the current page, and, with **Read page content** on, read a bookmarked page or the page you are on, and no other page. Once you have allowed access to all websites (for Check Dead Links on more than 20 sites, or for **Read page content**), it can also check whether bookmarked links still work. When you ask, it can also save a page, and move, rename, or delete bookmarks and create folders: those it first shows as a plan, and nothing changes until you approve it in the chat. After it has read a web page, it asks before saving too. Every change is one step you can undo, also with <Shortcut id="manager.undo" /> in the bookmark manager. Each step it takes appears in the chat.
- After some answers, buttons right above the message box suggest what to do next: save a page to a suggested folder, open or show a bookmark, or ask a follow-up question or pick one of a few options or ideas. They appear once the answer is complete, only the latest answer has them, and they step aside while you type and come back when the box is empty. Nothing happens until you click one, each works once, and a save can be undone from its notification.
- A save button names the folder and, below it, the page's title and site; point at it to see the full address. It only offers the page you are on, a page the AI found in your bookmarks or read, or an address you typed, never one that appears only in a web page's text. After the chat has read a web page or searched the web, clicking it first asks you to approve saving that page to that folder.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, and xAI can also search the web. Turn it on with the globe button next to the message box; it stays off until you do, and the provider runs the search itself.
- The conversation is kept only while the chat is open. **New chat** clears it, and closing the popup ends it.
- The instructions it follows are the **Ask AI** prompt in the prompt library, so you can view or replace them like any other prompt.
- **What Ask AI may do**, in **Settings**, then **AI**, lists every lookup and change it can make. Turn one off and Ask AI is not offered it at all. Applying a plan of changes always asks you first.
- For developers: AI agents on your computer, such as Claude Code, can use the same lookups and changes; see [Use with local AI agents](/guides/local-agents).

## See what was sent

To check exactly what a tool sent and what the provider answered, go to **Settings**, then **AI**, and turn on **Record requests** under **AI activity**. Each AI call then shows its source, address, status, and time; open one to see the request headers and the request and response bodies, or copy it as JSON for a bug report.

Recording is off by default. The log stays on this device and keeps only the most recent calls. API keys, headers whose names suggest a credential (such as ones containing `key`, `token`, `secret`, `auth`, or `cookie`), and every header you set in **Extra Headers JSON** are replaced with `[redacted]` before anything is stored, but bookmark titles and URLs in the requests are kept, so turn recording off when you are done: that also deletes the log.

## Save a page with a folder recommendation

Click the sparkle button in the popup. Choosing a suggested existing folder saves the page there. Choosing a suggested new folder opens **Review new folder** with the full path and the page; **Create Folder and Save** creates only the missing folders and saves the page. If the page is already in that folder it is not saved twice, and if a bookmark already uses one of the folder names in the path, nothing is created. **Cancel** changes nothing.

## Suggest tags and summaries

Run **Auto-Tagging** or **Content Summarizer** with **Analyze**. Suggestions are shown for review, and saved only when you click **Save Suggested Tags** or **Save Suggested Summaries**. **Merge Mode** decides whether saved suggestions are appended to what you have or replace it.

Saved tags and summaries live in this browser's local storage. You can also edit them by hand in **Bookmark Details**. They are not synced or searchable. A JSON bookmark export includes them, and importing that file gives them to the bookmarks it creates.

The tag count and style, summary length, and reorganization limits in Settings are sent to the provider as instructions; the provider's output is not checked against them.

## Reorganize folders

**AI Folder Reorganization** asks the provider which folder each bookmark belongs in. The plan always opens first as a list of bookmark moves, with moves into new folders marked and counted, and nothing changes until you click **Apply Changes**.

- **Apply Changes** moves the bookmarks and creates the new folders the plan names. If a folder with that name already exists in the same place, it is used instead.
- A bookmark you moved, gave a new URL, or deleted after the preview is skipped and left where it is, so the plan never overrides a newer change. When only part of the plan applies, the message counts the moved bookmarks, new folders, and skipped and failed items, and names the skipped bookmarks.
- Click **Undo** in the message or in the review, or press <Shortcut id="manager.undo" /> in the bookmark manager, to move the bookmarks back and remove the new folders that are still empty. A bookmark you moved again in the meantime stays where you put it.

## Export context for an AI chat

**AI Context Packer** exports the selected bookmarks as Markdown or XML that you can paste into any AI chat. It can include folder paths, dates, and your saved tags and summaries (**Include Saved Tags** and **Include Saved Summaries**). Like bookmark exports, it opens a privacy review when the file would contain values that may be private.

In Firefox, open the manager with **Open bookmark manager** in the toolbar popup to use the manager's AI tools. AI is not covered by the Firefox automated tests.
</Callout>
