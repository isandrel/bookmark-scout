## What you can do with it

- **Find**: search every bookmark by title or URL from the toolbar popup or the side panel, and keep named searches in the bookmarks manager. See [Search and saved searches](/guides/search).
- **Organize**: save the current page or a link into any folder, drag folders into shape, and delete with an undo. See [Save and organize](/guides/save-and-organize).
- **Clean up**: remove duplicate bookmarks, strip tracking parameters, and find and repair dead links. See [Clean up duplicates and tracking URLs](/guides/clean-up) and [Find and repair dead links](/guides/dead-links).
- **Move your library**: export to HTML, JSON, Markdown, or CSV, and import HTML or JSON after a preview. See [Import and export](/guides/import-export).
- **Use AI, only if you want to**: AI tools are off by default. When you turn them on, you pick the provider and bring your own key. See [Set up AI tools](/guides/ai-tools).

## Where your data goes

Bookmark Scout has no account and no server of its own. Your bookmarks stay in your browser. Data leaves the browser only when you use a feature that needs the network, such as checking dead links or an AI tool you turned on, and then it goes directly to the website or provider you chose. Read the [privacy summary](/privacy).

## Browsers

Chrome is the primary target. Edge is also Chromium-based and runs the same end-to-end test suite. In Firefox, which cannot replace its bookmarks page, the manager opens from a button in the popup, and a few browser features differ. See [Browser support](/browser-support) for the details.
